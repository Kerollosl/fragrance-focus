# Fragrance Focus design

Purpose: make each fragrance easy to understand while preserving Fragrantica's taste and playful personality.

Fragrantica is the visual authority. Keep its native fonts, logo/header, search, light/dark themes, colorful accord bars, designer logos, emoji voting, photographic notes, colored performance cards, gradients, and component shapes. Keep pros/cons and their original AI disclosure visible. Avoid an extension-branded dashboard or unlabeled gray charts.

Lead with the native title, a compact navigator, and one short takeaway in plain language. Prefer “Sweet vanilla” to a list of tags and fragrance taxonomy. Only infer wearing preferences from supported community votes; close, tied, and sparse votes must not become recommendations. Include two strongly supported seasons when appropriate. Retain one community star rating without a second visible copy below.

Give the bottle a generous, centered position in an equal-width two-column bottle/accord composition. Use a responsive image up to 300px wide, preserving its natural aspect ratio. Center the picture inside its column rather than leaving it at a grid edge. Do not manufacture empty vertical space with a fixed-height image box.

Keep native colorful reaction, season, longevity, sillage, gender, and value charts visible with all source labels and counts. Add short “Most votes” readouts directly to those cards so readers need not decode the result. Identify reaction percentages separately from star ratings. Compute approximate shares from visible counts, never normalized accord widths or the visitor's personal slider position. Hide only the personal performance sliders until requested; all original voting controls stay on their source nodes with their listeners intact.

Place “This perfume reminds me of” directly above Performance, following User Ratings and the personal-voting toggle. Group performance and value before notes, then pros/cons and reviews. Fold long descriptions and secondary discovery sections behind compact disclosures, preserving source content. Empty reactive wrappers must remain displayed for lazy loading. Recover extension UI that the site removes, deduplicate UI cloned by site hydration, and rebind cloned disclosure buttons.

Remove ads and affiliate offers. Do not override site-wide backgrounds, fonts, colors, gradients, or native title styles. Avoid decorative cards, repeated summaries, missing-data placeholders, invented hour estimates, and shopping recommendations. CSS reading-flow follows the visual order for keyboard and assistive navigation (Chrome 137+).

On narrow screens, retain the two-column bottle/accord arrangement, allow title wrapping, and scroll jump links within their own row. The note pyramid keeps centered stage headings, photographic links, and relative note emphasis. Section jumps account for the native sticky header. Restore original layout and all source visibility independently of ad removal.
